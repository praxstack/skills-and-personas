# Subject playbooks

The loop never changes. What changes per subject is the model behind the picture, the best first representation, and the misconceptions to expect. These are starting points, not rules.

## Mathematics

- **Model**: the function or construction itself. Let the learner drag the input and watch the output.
- **First picture**: geometry before algebra when there is any geometry (areas for completing the square, slopes for derivatives, rotations for complex multiplication).
- **Tools**: explorable with the formula shown live beside the figure; Manim for a transformation the learner should watch; JSXGraph for draggable constructions.
- **Watch for**: symbol-pushing without meaning. Check by asking what a term means in the picture.

## Computing, algorithms and DSA

- **Model**: the real algorithm, run on small inputs, with its state per step (the stack, the frontier, the pointers).
- **First picture**: the data structure at one moment, then the step that changes it.
- **Tools**: explorable whose figure is drawn from the running code's state; a code panel with the current line marked; concept-animation for one invariant holding as the loop runs. Use algo-sensei or techtutor for interview practice only when the learner asks to practise or be quizzed; an interview topic alone remains explanation-first.
- **Watch for**: pattern-matching a problem to a memorized template. Check with a variation that breaks the template.

## Systems, networking and ML

- **Model**: a small simulation with the knobs that matter (latency, cache size, learning rate, batch size).
- **First picture**: the request, packet or tensor travelling through the parts, with real units.
- **Tools**: explain-diagram for architecture; explorable for trade-offs ("drag the cache size, watch hit rate and cost"); concept-animation for one flow; Three.js for high-dimensional geometry only when 3D adds meaning.
- **Watch for**: boxes-and-arrows fluency without numbers. Ask for the order of magnitude.

## Physical sciences

- **Model**: the equations, or a numerical simulation of them, with the honest assumptions.
- **First picture**: the phenomenon (the beam on the ground, the ball's path), then the forces or quantities as marks over it.
- **Tools**: explorable with physical units on every slider; p5.js for particle and field simulations; Manim for derivations.
- **Watch for**: everyday intuitions that are wrong (heavier falls faster, seasons from distance). Put the misconception in the lesson and let the model break it.

## Life sciences and medicine

- **Model**: a process with stages and rates (an action potential, enzyme kinetics, population dynamics), or a structure with named parts.
- **First picture**: a labelled diagram at the right scale, with the scale shown.
- **Tools**: explain-diagram for structures; concept-animation for processes; real images from Wikimedia Commons with their licence.
- **Watch for**: teleology ("the cell wants to"). Name the mechanism.

## History, social sciences and the humanities

- **Model**: there's rarely an equation, so the model is the causal structure: actors, constraints, events, and the evidence for each link.
- **First picture**: a timeline or a causal map, with the claim and the source on each arrow.
- **Tools**: explain-diagram (timelines, causal maps, D2 for actors and relations); explorable for counterfactual "what if this constraint changed?" with the reasoning shown, clearly labelled as a thought experiment; real maps and documents.
- **Watch for**: one-cause stories and hindsight. Ask what people at the time could and couldn't know.

## Languages

- **Model**: the rule and its exceptions, or the sound and how it's made.
- **First picture**: a sentence diagrammed, or the mouth position for a sound.
- **Tools**: explain-diagram for structure; audio where the host can play it; optional spaced recall for vocabulary and forms after explanation, or practice-first only when the learner asks for it.
- **Watch for**: translating word for word. Teach chunks in context.

## Music and art

- **Model**: the pattern (intervals, rhythm, proportion, colour relationships).
- **First picture**: the pattern made visible: a piano roll, a waveform, a colour wheel with the chosen colours marked.
- **Tools**: explorable with sound when the host can play audio; concept-animation for a progression unfolding.

## Practical skills (cooking, finance, fitness, tools)

- **Model**: the variables the learner controls and what they change (heat and time, rate and years, load and reps).
- **First picture**: the outcome at two settings side by side.
- **Tools**: an explorable calculator with the reasoning shown, not a black box; a checklist still for procedures.
- **Watch for**: safety. Say plainly when something needs a professional, and never invent numbers for health or money.
