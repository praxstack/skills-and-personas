# v2.2 numeric and wording audit (four built lessons)

Run 2026-10-19 on branch `candidate/v2.2` at `ae9b06f`. Read-only for the product: nothing in `lessons/`, `package/` or `spec/` was changed. Scratch files are in `.state/final-audit/` (gitignored). `holdout/` and `plan/proposed/` were not read.

## Verdict

Every program output, fact value and drawn number is correct. I recompiled all 20 programs and recomputed every number with exact arithmetic, and none of them is wrong.

Three statements that contain numbers are false or misleading. All three are in the neuron lesson:

1. The explorer's tiny-z sentence says 0.8 × 1.5 "is rounded to the nearest double, 1.2000000000000002", and that this rounding is why z misses 0. Both parts are wrong (N1).
2. The `c.kround` claim text makes the same causal mistake (N2).
3. The IVE says the film answers its question at 0:55. The answer, a = 0.6, first appears at 1:25 (N3).

P1 count by lesson: switch-fallthrough 1, one-byte-four-views 3, factorial-stack 0, neuron 4. There are 8 P1 findings, 14 P2 and 9 P3.

## Method

- **Compiling and running.** I compiled every program with Apple clang 21.0.0 (`-std=c17 -Wall -Wextra -O0`) and ran it. I also compiled the switch programs and `ub.c` with gcc-16 16.2.0 (`-Wall -Wextra`). Every stdout matched `build/capture/*.stdout` and the `value` field of every fact. `ub.c` was compiled but never run, as the lesson says.
- **binary64 checks.** I used Python `fractions` and `decimal` at 60 digits for:
  - the representability of 0.8, 0.4, 0.2, 0.6 and 1.2;
  - the exact product of double(0.8) and 1.5, and whether it is a tie;
  - z for neuron.c and k1 to k3;
  - the counterfactual "t1 = double(1.2)" sum at the four tiny-z settings.
- **The neuron explorer grid.** I rebuilt all 861 settings (w2 from -2 to 2, b from -1 to 1, step 0.1, each value read with `strtod` of its decimal) as a C program, `.state/final-audit/grid.c`, compiled with `-ffp-contract=off`. FLT_EVAL_METHOD is 0. I compared it field by field with the `rows` tables in `neuron/build/explorer/index.html` and `ive/ive-data.js`. There were 0 differences. Exactly four nonzero values have |z| < 1e-9, at the four settings `c.kround` names, and no setting gives an exact 0.
- **Platform limits.** CHAR_BIT is 8, UCHAR_MAX is 255 and UINT_MAX is 4294967295. `__STDC_IEC_559__` is not defined.
- **Wording.** I collected every human-readable string from each lesson's `explorer`, `qa` and `cards` texts.json, both `captions.vtt` files, `chapters.vtt`, `transcript.txt`, `ive/cues.json`, the text and alt of every still SVG, and the visible text of each HTML page. That came to 195 to 266 unique strings per lesson (`.state/final-audit/texts-<slug>.txt`). I read all of them against the programs, the outputs and the stored passages, and looked at the four wide stills as images.
- **Claims not rendered.** Four claims, `c.dmem`, `c.kround`, `c.recap` (factorial) and `c.nomatch`/`c.bodyend`, exist in lesson.json but their exact text is not rendered anywhere; a variant is rendered instead. They are still audited as teaching sentences.

## Numeric results (all correct unless listed under findings)

- **switch-fallthrough.**
  - The outputs are two/three/done, two/done and two/done.
  - Every line number in the claims, the trace paths and the explorer's computed rows for n = 0 to 5, with and without breaks, matches the three sources.
  - gcc-16 warns at fallthrough.c 7:9 and 9:9, warns only at 7:9 for into_break.c, and says nothing for with_break.c. Clang `-Wextra` is silent for all three.
- **one-byte-four-views.**
  - 66 is 01000010 / 0x42 / 'B'. 200 is 11001000 / 0xC8, and %c gives byte 200. -1 is 11111111 / 0xFF, %u gives 4294967295 (-1 + 2^32), and %c gives byte 255. 321 = 256 + 65 is 01000001 / 0x41, and %c gives byte 65 ('A').
  - (double)66 = 1.03125 × 2^6. The biased exponent is 1029 = 0x405 and the fraction is 2^-5, so the bits are 0x4050800000000000. In little-endian byte order that is 00 00 00 00 00 80 50 40, and the int's bytes are 42 00 00 00.
  - Each of 66, 200, -1 and 321 is exact in double, so %f prints them with six zeros after the point.
- **factorial-stack.**
  - n = 1 to 5 give main got 1, 2, 6, 24 and 120. The entry and exit orders are correct.
  - Lines 4 to 9 and 13 to 14 are correct. The explorer's `r = 4 * 6`, `r = 5 * 24` and the other slot values are correct.
- **neuron.**
  - 0.8, 0.4, 0.2, 0.6 and 1.2 are not exact in binary64; 1.5 is.
  - t1 = 1.20000000000000017763568394002504646778106689453125. That is the smallest double above 1.2, and `%+g` prints +1.2.
  - z = 0.600000000000000088817841970012523233890533447265625. That is the smallest double above 0.6, and `%g` prints 0.6. `z == 0.6` is false.
  - k1: z = -0.19999999999999984 prints as -0.2, and a = +0. k2: z = 1.7000000000000002 prints as 1.7. k3: z = -0.5999999999999999 prints as -0.6, and a = 0.
  - The `%+g` reasoning holds: P = 6, X = 0 gives precision 5, and X = -1 gives precision 6.
  - In the still, I checked every axis position in pixels: 0.6, 1.2 and 0.8 on the add axis, and 0.6 on the gate axis.

### P3: style

- **switch, `c.clang`.** It is true. I measured it: clang 21 with -Wall -Wextra is silent. But it leaves out that clang's `-Wimplicit-fallthrough`, which -Wextra does not enable, flags both fallthroughs in into_break.c, including the line 9 to 10 case that gcc-16 misses. That is a useful contrast for sc7.
- **switch, card r:c.break.** "Here that is the switch, so execution continues after it": the second sentence repeats the S4 issue in the true/false front.
- **one-byte, `c.recap` / sc8.** "(double)a changes both the type and the bits" has no "on this machine". Here the sizes alone differ (4 and 8 bytes), so the sentence is true, but `c.cast` keeps the qualifier and this doesn't.
- **one-byte, `c.k200.c`, `c.km1.c`, `c.k321.c`, `c.k321.byte`.** "unsigned char holds 0 to 255" and "8-bit bytes" are true (CHAR_BIT 8 and UCHAR_MAX 255 measured here), but no lesson fact captures them.
- **factorial, `c.waits` justification.** It cites §6.5.2.2 ¶5, which is not stored. The stored passage is ¶11. `sources/n2310-6.8.6.3p2.txt` (break) is cited by no claim.
- **neuron, `sources/n2310-7.12.7.5p2.txt` (sqrt).** No claim cites it.
- **neuron, `c.mul`.** "0.8 × 1.5 prints as +1.2 and 0.4 × -1 = -0.4" mixes "prints as" with "=". Later the lesson stresses inexact doubles, and t2 is -0.400000000000000022…. Use "prints as" for both.
- **neuron, `c.toy`.** On the still and its alt, "The program … prints them with %g or %+g" names no program; double_format.c prints with %d. The still shows only neuron.c, so this is P3 there.
- **All lessons.** Passages are taken from N2310 but cited as "C17, working draft N2176". Every excerpt file's header says so, so this is disclosed, not a mismatch. Every quote I checked matches its stored passage word for word.

**Where I grade lower:**
- **Scope rejections where the code shown on the same card, panel or step already scopes the sentence.** Examples are switch `c.setup`/`c.fall` on q:sc2 and the main panel, and factorial `c.depth`/`c.main` at the explorer steps. I call these P2, not false.
