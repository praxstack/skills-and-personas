# Learning-platform inspiration for a general teaching skill

Reviewed 2026-10-16. Scope: Brilliant, Educative, Imprint, Nibble, Wondering. No accounts, purchases, publishing, or implementation. These are design references, not evidence that a proposed teaching skill improves learning.

## Recommendation

Combine **Brilliant's model → prediction → explanation → formalization**, **Educative's readable course spine with nearby practice and notes**, **Imprint's compact explanatory visuals**, **Nibble's learner-selected formats**, and **Wondering's explicit goal/depth/background controls**. Retain the causal teaching sequence independently of any browser, canvas, code runner, or proprietary tutor. Recommendations below are design inferences, not measured learning effects.

## Coverage and evidence limits

Official sources were searched with Exa and web search, then direct pages were read. Four research questions: how lessons produce intuition; how visual actions encode concepts; how learners control depth/pacing; how courses and notes preserve continuity. Public page text and documentation were inspected; **no interactive lesson was completed and no rendered UI behavior was verified**. An in-app-browser inspection attempt failed because that browser was unavailable. Product screenshots described in page metadata are not counted as live UI observations. Marketing testimonials, ratings, user counts, and claims of faster learning do not establish efficacy. No independent outcome studies were assessed in this bounded platform review.

## Platform findings

| Platform | What the official evidence supports | Adaptation and limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Brilliant** | Its educator guide describes short interactive lessons, skills checks, grouped levels and reviews. A concrete algebra sequence asks learners to predict changes to a balanced scale, explain preserved equality, then translate that action into equation notation. It also proposes comparing alternate transformation strategies. This is instructional documentation, not our own lesson execution. [Educator guide](https://brilliant.org/help/schools-and-educators/how-brilliant-fits-into-a-math-lesson/) | Adopt an observable invariant and an explicit bridge from experience to symbols. Invite a prediction before revealing an outcome, but allow the learner to request explanation immediately. Avoid making every topic into a game or requiring repeated gates. |
| **Educative** | The author guide separates text, executable code, exercises, media, and utilities. Its learner FAQ documents selecting passage text to highlight or attach a note. These are documented features; persistence was not tested. [Widgets](https://www.educative.io/courses/author-guide/qAkxV39X3OD), [Learner FAQ](https://www.educative.io/faq/general-faq) | Use a navigable written explanation with a relevant experiment or question beside the point it teaches. Keep learner notes anchored to concepts. Generalize beyond coding: compare evidence, classify examples, sequence mechanisms, critique arguments. Avoid implementing a large widget inventory before knowing the teaching need. |
| **Imprint** | Its site markets concept-focused visuals plus saving and reviewing ideas; its longer introduction describes short visual pieces, book guides, original material and courses. Neither page establishes how well retention works. [Homepage](https://www.imprintapp.com/), [Product introduction](https://www.imprintapp.com/i/start-learning-popular-books-and-topics) | Adopt one explanatory relationship per visual, with a short caption and a reusable idea card. Preserve assumptions and exceptions in expandable depth. Avoid treating an attractive illustration or compressed book summary as evidence of deep understanding. |
| **Nibble** | Its site describes selectable topics and five formats: lessons with questions, historical-personality chat, video, games and audio. It advertises sessions of roughly 3–10 minutes and switching formats to suit available time. This is product marketing, not observed learning behavior. [Official site](https://nibble-app.com/) | Offer a quick entry and the learner's preferred presentation format, while keeping the same concept and explanation available. A short session should reach a coherent stopping point, not impose a depth ceiling. Avoid trivia-feed design, novelty switching that interrupts the explanation, or presenting simulated historical dialogue as sourced testimony. |
| **Wondering** | Official public material describes tailored short courses, visuals, practice and goal/background personalization. Search-rendered homepage content exposes basics/deep/practical goals and time/focus choices. A directly fetched public course shows four sections and 25 lessons progressing from core models to application. The developer's App Store description also advertises knowledge cards. [Homepage](https://wondering.app/), [Public course](https://wondering.app/learn/mental-models-toolkit-b15d7e56c3ddf817a21d7a16), [Developer listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/wondering-learn-anything/id6754904253) | Ask for the learner's purpose and relevant prior knowledge, show an editable path, and allow changes in depth. Distinguish an attractive generated outline from verified prerequisites, correct content, and genuine mastery. Do not infer a fixed “learning style” from a format preference. |

## Concrete patterns to carry into the skill

These proposals transfer the design ideas; their causal explanations are hypotheses to test, not claims proven by these sites.

1. **Start with a question that needs the concept.** Present a concrete situation, contrast or anomaly. Let the learner notice or predict something. Then explain the mechanism and introduce terminology. Brilliant's scale example provides an unusually explicit instance of this progression. The hypothesized benefit is that notation names a relationship the learner has already encountered. [Brilliant guide](https://brilliant.org/help/schools-and-educators/how-brilliant-fits-into-a-math-lesson/)
2. **Make interaction change meaning.** A slider should alter a relevant quantity; a drag should change a relation; a button should expose a counterexample. If nothing conceptual changes, a labeled static diagram may be better. Brilliant's design account emphasizes difficulty progression, variation and edge cases, with human review of generated problems. Its historical 2025 position on chatbots must not be confused with its current homepage, which now markets Koji tutoring. [Design account](https://blog.brilliant.org/hand-crafted-machine-made/), [Current homepage](https://brilliant.org/)
3. **Keep learner control explicit.** Offer “explain directly,” “give a hint,” “another example,” “show the formal version,” “let me try,” and “go deeper.” Treat these as options, not a mandatory menu after every paragraph. Wondering and Nibble supply inspiration for goal and format choice; the particular controls proposed here are our design recommendation. [Wondering](https://wondering.app/), [Nibble](https://nibble-app.com/)
4. **Use checks to diagnose reasoning.** Ask why an answer works, what changes under a changed assumption, or where an analogy breaks. Give feedback on the reasoning and permit skipping. A correct multiple-choice answer alone should not trigger a mastery claim. Educative documents several exercise families; Brilliant describes explaining strategies and review checks. [Educative widgets](https://www.educative.io/courses/author-guide/qAkxV39X3OD), [Brilliant guide](https://brilliant.org/help/schools-and-educators/how-brilliant-fits-into-a-math-lesson/)
5. **Preserve both a course map and useful notes.** Recommended note unit: concept → intuitive model → precise statement → worked example → limitation/counterexample → learner question → source. Link it to prerequisites and a suggested next step. Educative supports contextual annotation, Imprint markets saved ideas, and Wondering exposes course structure/cards; the combined portable note schema is our proposal. [Educative FAQ](https://www.educative.io/faq/general-faq), [Imprint](https://www.imprintapp.com/), [Wondering listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/wondering-learn-anything/id6754904253)
6. **Keep the teaching contract portable.** The essential deliverable is the explanation, causal relationship, example, learner choice, and durable notes. A capable harness may render a simulation; a text-only harness can use a small table, diagram, worked trace, or prediction with a revealed result. This is an architectural inference for the requested harness-independent skill, not a claim about the platforms.

## Suggested evaluation examples, not new mandatory gates

- Algebra: predict what happens when only one scale side changes; explain equality; derive the permitted operation; transfer to a symbolic equation.
- History: compare two explanations for one event, inspect timeline/evidence, separate causal inference from chronology, and identify a counterexample.
- Programming: predict a short program's output, trace changing state, explain the invariant, then change one condition.
- Philosophy: use a concrete case, state an argument, test a counterexample, and mark the analogy's limits.

Observe whether a learner can explain the mechanism and transfer it to a changed case. Also check whether they can request direct explanation, skip practice, change depth, and retrieve coherent notes. Do not use clicks, completion, streaks, time spent, or aesthetic polish as substitutes for understanding.

## Fit to the requested explain-first, hand-drawn teaching style

Brilliant's current homepage says its tutor does not simply supply answers. That product stance should **not** become a compulsory Socratic rule here: give a clear explanation when requested and make predictions/practice optional. Borrow the causal model and targeted help, not answer withholding. [Current Brilliant homepage](https://brilliant.org/)

For the user's requested live whiteboard default, draw only the part of the model currently being explained, use persistent visual anchors, and reveal relationships in the order the prose explains them. Keep readable labels, replay/step controls where supported, and a complete static fallback. Hand-drawn appearance is a user-directed presentation choice; this review did not establish it as a feature or learning advantage of all five products.

## Remaining gaps

Actual onboarding friction, hint quality, wrong-answer recovery, keyboard accessibility, visual clarity, note export, and cross-session persistence remain untested across all five. Imprint and Nibble offer weaker publicly inspected evidence about deep conceptual sequencing than Brilliant's educator guide. Wondering's public outline demonstrates organization only; no generated lesson correctness was audited. A later authorized trial could inspect one matched concept end to end on each service, but this research does not require paid access to inform the skill's design.
