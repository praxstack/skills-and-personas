Each row has a line number, the case label chip and the statement chip, a red segment where control falls through, and output arrows (`S/frames/switch-fallthrough/t0046.40.png`).
- **One-byte draws all 4 bytes of a.** The still and film show `42 00 00 00` with +0 to +3, a leader from the 42 cell to the bit tape, and the 8 bytes of the double read from the highest address down. I checked the bits, the byte order and the hex. All are correct.
- **The factorial still marks returned frames as popped,** with return arrows into the `[ ]` slots and the lede "The calls start in the order 3, 2, 1, and they finish in the reverse order: 1, 2, 3."
- **Neuron explorer surprises are now taught.**
  - The caption follows the sliders ("With w2 = -1 and b = -0.8, z prints as 1.11022e-16 ...").
  - 1.11022e-16 now gets its own explanation (`S/interact/neuron-explorer-dark-i10-j2.png`).
- **Narration is calmer and plainer.**
  - Every cue now runs at 83 to 156 wpm. In rc1, 13 of 48 cues ran above 195.
  - "unsigned car" is now "the cast to unsigned".
  - The hedges are mostly gone from the narration: "on this machine" is said once, in one-byte sc1.1.
  - The neuron recap no longer repeats its opening sentence.
- **Switch truth gaps are closed.** The gcc-16 `-Wextra` warnings at lines 7 and 9, gcc's silence for `into_break.c` line 9, and Apple clang's silence are all claimed from recorded diagnostics (`lesson.json` c.warn, c.clang).

## Teaching behaviour: is it explanation first?

Mostly in the film. Not yet in the Q&A, and not fully in the IVE.

- **Film and IVE default play: explanation first. Pass.**
  - In default mode the IVE plays straight through every question: 3.5 s after starting 1 s before `pause_at`, the film was still playing, on all four lessons at both widths (`S/interact/log.json`, `default_after_3_5s`).
  - The MP4 has no think gap: 0.8 s or less between the question cue and the answer scene.
  - Practice mode is off by default. When turned on, it stops the film within 0.03 s of `pause_at` and shows the options (`*-ive-practice-ask-*`).
- **IVE leftovers: a soft prediction barrier in default mode.**
  - Pausing before the answer locks the live controls: "These controls unlock after the film answers its question, at 0:20" (`S/interact/*-ive-explore-early-1440.png`, `explore_early` in the log).
  - Some chapter titles still read "Opens after the answer" before the film passes the answer (switch 0:26 and 0:37, one-byte 0:47 and 1:56, factorial 0:50 and 1:20, neuron 1:11).
  - Neither should apply when practice mode is off.
- **The question scene in the film is a dead interstitial.**
  - The question is printed but never spoken. The narrator says only "The scenes that follow answer this question" (switch sc2.0), or "The next scene answers this question" (one-byte sc3.0, which is 2.5 s long).
  - A listener hears a reference to a question they were never asked.
- **Q&A: still question first. Fail.**
  - Layer 1 is "The question" plus the program. Every later layer leads with a question and hides its explanation behind "Reveal".
  - The scenes before the question are missing altogether:
    - one-byte drops "The int and its low byte" and "Two integer views";
    - factorial drops sc1 to sc3 (the frames being pushed);
    - neuron drops Multiply;
    - switch drops its setup scene.
  - The Q&A has no figure at all, only text and output blocks (`S/interact/*-qa-revealed.txt`, `.png`).
- **Explorers: no barrier. Pass.**
  - One-byte, factorial and neuron open on "Step through the run" with the explanation beside it. Switch opens on the knob.
  - Nothing is gated.

## Blind-spot inspection (b0-measures.md, Parked)

`S/cov.py` splits each image into three groups:
- ground;
- flat fill: any single colour other than the ground covering 1% or more, plus colours within 4 levels of it;
- ink: everything else.

It then dilates by 6 px. The gap between dilated non-ground and dilated ink-only is the share of coverage that card fills alone provide. Stills were measured below the header (top 20% cut). Frames were measured whole.

| Spot | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Fill-only cards and dilation inflate coverage | **Found. Borderline in the stills, real in sparse film scenes.** | See below. |
| 2. CSS generated text | **Not found** | No non-empty `::before`/`::after` content on any of the 64 page loads (`S/pages/info.json`, `gen` is empty everywhere). The still SVGs have no `content:` at all. The film compositions only use `justify-/align-content:center`. |
| 3. Faint texture | **Not found** | No `<pattern>` and no `background-image` on any page. Near-ground faint pixels are 0.07 to 0.25% in the stills and 0.5 to 1.7% in the frames. Textured near-ground 12 px blocks are 0.2 to 3.3%, consistent with anti-aliasing at glyph edges and H.264 noise. No empty area hides behind a texture. |
| 4. Values just outside the mechanism unchecked | **Found. Still legitimate, with one formatting miss.** | See below. |
| 5. Widely spaced code | **Found. Legitimate for switch, borderline for one-byte.** | See below. |

**Spot 1 in detail.**
- In the stills, fills add about 24 points for factorial: dilated non-ground is 50.6%, against 26.1% for dilated ink. The stack-frame surfaces are mechanism, but the tinted wrong-belief card is fill around about 15 words. Fills add about 6 points for neuron, 4 for switch and 0 for one-byte.
- In the films, card fills cover 20 to 49% of the frame and add 17 to 43 points:
  - switch sc7: 43 points (`t0103.50.png`), where a 2-line output card fills a quarter of the frame;
  - one-byte sc1: 32 points (`t0012.83.png`), where the output card holds 2 lines in about 330 px of height;
  - factorial: 31 points;
  - neuron sc1: 17 points.
- The earlier verdict ("card fills carry 15 to 25 points of film coverage") **still holds for the stills but understates the films.** In the sparse scenes, fills carry 30 to 43 points.

**Spot 4 in detail.**
- Every digit-bearing text in the four wide stills carries `data-fact`, `data-claim`, `data-src-line` or `data-label`. The only unbound ones are the two wrong-belief sentences (`.state/review-b12/walk.py` run on `*-wide-dark.svg`).
- I hand-checked the values in 26 frames and in all the explorer states I drove. All are right.
- The one exception is a formatting miss rather than a wrong value: neuron's output line 3 loses its double space. In the Q&A, factorial's output lines also run together onto one line, "enter factorial(3) enter factorial(2) enter factorial(1)" (`S/interact/factorial-stack-qa-revealed.png`), so the output's line structure is not shown faithfully.

**Spot 5 in detail.**
- Switch path chips sit about 4x the font size apart in the still and in film sc3 to sc5. They are real path nodes, so this is legitimate, but it is the exact pattern the check misses.
- One-byte film sc1 to sc3 shows four printf lanes at about 4.3x the font size, empty while they wait for their values (`t0012.83.png`). That is widely spaced code with nothing in it.
- The film code panels are normal: 1.5 to 2.1x.

## Top 5 improvements per lesson

### switch-fallthrough
1. **Keep the path figure in scenes 6 and 7.** Show `with_break.c` and `into_break.c` as their own path figures beside fallthrough.c's ghost route, not as code plus a paragraph.
2. **Put the gcc diagnostics on screen in sc6 and sc7.** Show the two `warning: this statement may fall through` lines pinned to lines 7 and 9, and an empty slot for line 9 of `into_break.c`, so the "silent" claim is seen.
3. **In scenes 3 to 5, drop either the code panel's path or the figure.** One path per frame. Use the freed space to make the figure bigger.
4. **Speak the question, or cut the question scene.** Better still, open sc2 on "Here is what to watch: does execution stop at case 3?" and go straight to the match.
5. **Use one visual grammar in the explorer.** Give it the still's gutter line numbers and chip structure, so the knob feels like the same figure moving.

### one-byte-four-views
1. **Give each film scene its own frame.** Show only the lane being explained, large, and fade the finished lanes to a compact strip. Today sc4 to sc8 are one crowded board.
2. **Fix the type hierarchy.** Values first and largest, labels small caps under them. Never wrap "LOW BYTE · BITS AND HEX". Show `0x4050800000000000` once.
3. **Relabel the 8-byte row** "d = (double)a · 8 bytes" in the still, the film and the explorer. Say once that the cast makes a value, which `views.c` line 15 stores in `d`.
4. **Replace the meta lede** with the insight: "%f gets a new double, 66.0, whose bits look nothing like a's." Give the question scene enough time, or fold it into sc4.
5. **Put the explorer's comparison table into the film's recap.** It is the strongest picture in the lesson, and the recap currently repeats the sc7 frame.

### factorial-stack
1. **Replace the code-path arc tangle** with a per-frame current-line marker: one coloured caret per active frame, matching the frame's colour.
2. **Make the stack the hero of the film.** Give it about 60% of the width, trim the code panel to lines 3 to 15 at the top with no empty band, and keep the recap stack the same size as in the scenes before it.

### neuron
