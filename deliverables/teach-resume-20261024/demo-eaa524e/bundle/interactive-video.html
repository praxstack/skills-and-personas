Transcript

  1. Start with the batch. The hypothetical batch has 100 defective items and 9,900 sound items. Each rate starts with its own population.
  2. Select within defective items. The detector flags 90 of the 100 defective items. Its 90% hit rate leaves 10 defective items missed.
  3. Select within sound items. Five percent of 9,900 sound items gives 495 false flags. A smaller rate can produce more flags because its population is larger.
  4. Collect only flags. Move the 90 true flags and 495 false flags into one group. Leave 9,415 unflagged items outside, including 10 missed defects.
  5. Change the denominator. Expand the same selected group. Its count stays 585. The defective share is 90 divided by 585, or 15.4%.
  6. Change the base rate. At a 10% defect rate, the same detector gives 900 true flags and 450 false flags. Of 1,350 flags, 66.7% are defective.
  7. Remove false flags. With zero false flags, all 900 flags are true. Yet 100 defective items remain missed. Precision is 100%; hit rate is still 90%.
